## Summary

Your Funding paragraph follows the official BCI statement on the [ForestGEO data-use terms page](https://ctfs.si.edu/datarequest/index.php/request/terms/1) almost word for word, so it inherits that statement's three faulty NSF numbers. The published literature also credits people and funders the draft leaves out.

**Fix before release**

- **Grant numbers:** DEB-00753102 should be DEB-0075102; DEB-9909347 should be DEB-9909947; DEB-9221033 has no NSF record (Hubbell's award with that stem is DEB-9221032). Add DEB-0948585, the 2010 recensus grant. Details under NSF grant numbers below.
- **Funder name:** write "Andrew W. Mellon Foundation" in full, as [Hubbell et al. 1999](https://faculty.lsu.edu/kharms/files/hubbell_etal_1999.pdf) and [Comita et al. 2010](https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e5cadbe3-a828-49bc-bf7e-0f3565607d0a/content) do.
- **Team name:** "Richar  Martínez" has a double space, and [ForestGEO's 2020 census team list](https://forestgeo.si.edu/node/146977) spells him "Richard Martínez". Please confirm.

**Consider adding**

- **Key people:** Robin Foster (co-founder, first botanist), Rolando Pérez and Salomón Aguilar (species identification, field management since 1995), Suzanne Lao (data management) and Steven Dolins (database design). The PIs' own [2012 data release](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/20925?show=full) asked users to credit all five.
- **Recent and current funders:** the HSBC Climate Partnership (2007–2011) and the Smithsonian Institution Global Earth Observatories, both credited in [Condit et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0049826). The [2024 history chapter](https://doi.org/10.5479/si.26809612) adds that NSF was the primary funder of the 1995–2010 censuses and the Smithsonian took over after 2010. Your NSF-first Funding paragraph does not show this; the suggested text below does.
- **Founding partner:** Princeton University, which [established the plot with STRI in 1980](https://forestgeo.si.edu/sites/neotropics/barro-colorado-island).
- **Historic funders** from 1980s–1990s papers: eight foundations and programs not in the draft. The 2024 chapter confirms that Earthwatch volunteer teams paid for most of the 1985 census and a Smithsonian Scholarly Studies grant saved the 1990 census (see the comparison table).
- **Census dates and leads:** [ForestGEO reports](https://forestgeo.si.edu/node/146977) the ninth census began in January 2020, paused for COVID-19 and resumed on 14 February 2022, led by Salomón Aguilar, Rolando Pérez and David Mitre. Maikol Guevara also appears in the 2020 team. None of these four is in your list, unless they are authors.
- **Climate data:** if the release includes the STRI Physical Monitoring Program series (Paton 2026), that program asks for an acknowledgment (see Data-use requirements).

One open question: the Frank H. Levinson Family Foundation is in your draft but not in the official BCI statement. It funds the ForestGEO network ([Davies et al. 2021](https://aura.abdn.ac.uk/items/a01ed892-b99f-435a-ad32-006755fc801e/full)) and funded BCI trait data ([Rüger et al. 2018](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_2608755_3/component/file_2608753/BGC2872.pdf)); keep it if it supported the census.

**Funding.** The Barro Colorado Island (BCI) forest dynamics research project was made possible by National Science Foundation grants to Stephen P. Hubbell: DEB-0640386, DEB-0425651, DEB-0346488, DEB-0129874, DEB-00753102, DEB-9909347, DEB-9615226, DEB-9405933, DEB-9221033, DEB-9100058, DEB-8906869, DEB-8605042, DEB-8206992 and DEB-7922197. Since the 2015 census, the project's primary funder has been the Smithsonian Institution, through the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute (STRI) and the Forest Global Earth Observatory (ForestGEO; formerly the Center for Tropical Forest Science and the Smithsonian Institution Global Earth Observatories). We acknowledge additional support from the HSBC Climate Partnership, the John D. and Catherine T. MacArthur Foundation, the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, the Frank H. Levinson Family Foundation, the Small World Institute Fund and numerous private individuals. In its first two decades the project was also supported by the Smithsonian Scholarly Studies Program, the World Wildlife Fund, the Earthwatch Center for Field Studies, the National Geographic Society, the Geraldine R. Dodge Foundation, the W. Alton Jones Foundation, the Pew Charitable Trusts and the Guggenheim Foundation. The plot is part of ForestGEO, a global network of large-scale forest dynamics plots.

**Field stations, marine laboratories and related research institutions.** The data were collected in the Barro Colorado Nature Monument, Panama, at the Barro Colorado Island research station of the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute (9.15° N, 79.85° W). The plot was established in 1980 by the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute and Princeton University. We thank the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute for logistical support.

**Additional acknowledgments.** We thank Robin B. Foster, who co-founded the plot with Stephen P. Hubbell and led its botany through the first censuses; Richard Condit, data manager from 1991 and head of the project from 1995 to 2010; Steven Dolins for database design; and Allen Herre, Jeff Klahn, Sue Williams and Zenith Batista, who led field work in the 1980s and the 1990 census. We also thank the hundreds of field assistants, botanists and data managers who have censused the plot since 1981, and the field team that completed the ninth census in 2022–2023, after its 2020 start was interrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic: Richar Martínez, Victor Rojas, Luis Navarro, Luis Alfredo Martínez, Roni Saenz, Guillermo Aguilar, Luis Aguilar, Biancolini Castro, Anayansi Cerezo, Luis Cedeño, Tamara Ubarte, Reina Bermudez and Eliecer Alvarado. Meteorological data were provided by the Physical Monitoring Program of the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute.
