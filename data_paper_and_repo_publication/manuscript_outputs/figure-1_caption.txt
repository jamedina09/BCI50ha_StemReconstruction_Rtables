Figure 1. Trees of the Barro Colorado Island 50-ha plot through nine censuses, 1982–2023. A tree is
one individual, whatever its number of stems. In each census, every tree recorded alive up to then
is in one of three groups, from the census tables: with a living stem at 1.3 m, that is, a stem
with Rstatus A on a record that is not a stump (above zero; total written above the line; these are
the trees counted in a census); alive without a living stem at 1.3 m (grey, next to zero): broken
below 1.3 m, with a record whose DFstatus is broken below and that has no diameter (a stump, which
the field crew recorded alive), or, for a few trees, missing or recorded dead in that census and
alive in a later one; and dead (pale colours, cumulative): no living stem at 1.3 m and no
broken-below record in that census or in any later one. Blue: the 235,336 trees of census 1;
orange: trees first recorded alive after census 1. Percentages: share of the trees of the same
group, recorded alive up to that census, that have a living stem at 1.3 m (above zero) or are dead
(below zero); the rest of each group is in the grey band, whose number of trees is written in it;
percentages are rounded to 0.1%, so the three shares of a group add up to 100% within 0.1%. In
2022–2023, 203,489 trees had a living stem at 1.3 m (73,445 of census 1, 31.2%; 130,044 new,
57.9%), 13,536 were broken below (3,880 of census 1, 9,656 new) and 242,921 were dead (158,011 of
census 1, 67.1%; 84,910 new, 37.8%). From census 2 to 8, the grey band holds 5,968–14,646 trees,
88–99% of them broken below. In the census tables, the stump records of 13,536 of the 13,536
broken-below trees of 2022–2023 are coded A (alive, without a diameter). Shaded vertical bands:
period in which 95% of the records of each census were taken (2.5% to 97.5% quantiles of the record
dates); each census is placed at its median record date. 265 trees with only dead or missing
records are not shown.
